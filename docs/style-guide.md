# Masar Visual Style Guide

Masar's figures are drawn in loose black brush ink and filled with one warm oat colour and solid black. They sit on a neutral grey page, with precise geometric type and hairline-thin UI outlines. The style comes from the contrast between hand-drawn figures and exact typography.

The closest lineage is Pablo Stanley's Open Peeps, redrawn with a heavier brush line. As in Jean Jullien's work, a single flat fill is laid in after the ink. It belongs to the hand-drawn "Notion-style" family that grew up as a reaction against Corporate Memphis. Shorthand: **ink + oat**.

*Reference: a Canva template, "How to Improve Focus and Concentration While Studying" (a cover and 8 slides, seen on a Pinterest board). The creator is unknown. Each content slide is only about 314 px wide in the source, so every measurement here is approximate and scaled to a 1920×1080 slide. Slides are referred to as `cover` and `s1`–`s8`, in reading order. Masar takes the principles, never the drawings (see 7.4).*

> **Decisions marked *Proposed* are recommendations awaiting sign-off:** the blue accent, retiring the proposal's serif, and the logo rules.

---

## 1. The idea

| Principle | On the page |
|---|---|
| Three values, one hand | Paper, oat, ink. Ink goes on things that are dark in life: hair, suits, ties, devices, coffee. Where there is a choice, black is placed for balance (Dow's *notan*). Oat covers everything else, including white shirts. |
| Warmth is in the people | The page is neutral. Oat on paper is only 1.21:1, so the line does the work of separating figure from page. The oat adds warmth. |
| Simple faces invite identification | McCloud's "amplification through simplification": the less specific a face, the more viewers see themselves in it. Yang et al. (2024) tested this in science comics. Everyone has the same skin colour; dress and features carry identity. |
| Leave it open | Inner lines stop short, and the eye closes them (Gestalt closure). |
| Gesture first | Big hands tell the story. Figures cropped by the frame or seen from behind pull the viewer in. |
| Hierarchy by size, not weight | Titles are regular weight and the UI lines are hairline-thin. The drawn line is the only expressive mark. |
| Visibly handmade | It reads as human, and in 2026 as "not AI". |

**Register:** calm, intelligent, approachable, never salesy. Think of a good teacher's whiteboard, not an ad.

| Audience | What they should feel | Risk → guard |
|---|---|---|
| Students | "This is for me, not an ad." | They may recognise the Canva figures → use only Masar's own figures |
| Parents | A counsellor, not a salesman | It can look unserious for an expensive decision → pair it with proof: numbers, university names, black-and-white event photos |
| Universities | Restrained and editorial; their logo is the only colour on the page | It can look like a template → illustrate openers and process diagrams only, never pricing or data |

## 2. Colour

| Token | Hex | Role | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `paper` | `#F0F0F0` | Every background | Measured `#F0F0F0`–`#F1F1F1` |
| `oat` | `#E5DAD0` | The only fill: skin, clothes (white garments too), light hair, props | Measured `#DDD4CD`–`#E7DAD3` |
| `ink` | `#111111` | Lines, solid masses, all type | Measured `#000000`–`#080808` in solid areas. `#111111` is a deliberate softening for screen and print |
| `hairline` | `#9B9B9B` | Outlines of decorative pills and callouts | Estimated: the reference stroke is thinner than a pixel and blurred |
| `hairline-strong` | `#8A8A8A` | Borders of interactive pills and inputs on the web (3.03:1, meets WCAG) | Derived |
| `masar-blue` | `#1AA0E6` | The path signal | Masar brand, not in the reference |

The dark brown `#241F1C` is the Pinterest board behind the slides, not a Masar colour.

**Ratios.** Measured by warmth, the reference is about 75% paper, 16% oat and 9% ink (oat per slide runs from 9% on s7 to 23% on s8). Target per frame: paper ≥ 65%, oat 8–25%, ink 5–15%, blue ≤ 2%.

**Contrast**

| Pair | Ratio | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| ink on paper | 16.6:1 | Body text is safe |
| ink on oat | 13.7:1 | Text on oat is safe |
| oat on paper | 1.21:1 | Oat is decorative only |
| blue on paper | 2.55:1 | Never use blue for text |
| ink on blue | 6.49:1 | A blue button carries ink text |

**Decision (*Proposed*): Masar blue marks the path, nothing else.**
- At most one blue element per frame: either the route line or the single main call to action.
- Never inside illustrations, and never as a background, tint or gradient.
- Never as text.
- Never on frames that carry a partner university's logo.
- GET ED and Eshra7ly colours appear only inside their own logos.

**Never:**
- a second fill colour, gradients, shadows or grain
- tints on screen (print is the one exception, below)
- varied skin tones
- coloured type
- a cream page: on `#EDE9E2`, oat drops to 1.14:1
- illustrations on dark backgrounds (full-ink pages are for black-and-white photos and section dividers only)
- the white halos around lines in the reference crops (they are JPEG artefacts)

**Print.** Choose one of two options:
- Flood K5–6 as the paper colour. Proof it, because it can look dirty.
- Print on white stock and treat white as paper.

Either way, proof oat against the paper you chose. A starting point for oat is C0 M5 Y9 K10; match it to a Pantone on a physical proof.

## 3. Illustration

**Line.** Near-black and near-even in weight, with a gentle brush-pen swell and rounded, slightly tapered ends. Strokes are long and wobble slowly.

**Fill**
- Every area is oat or solid ink. Ink is used for dark hair, beards, ties, trousers, dark jackets, laptops, pens, coffee and marks.
- Fill mostly fits figures but may spill loosely past hair and body outlines (s2, s3). It fits exactly on faces and hands.
- On props the fill runs loose. The whiteboard is an oat block outlined on 2–3 partial edges, and its left edge is a raw fill edge.
- Treat fills as flat. Faint grain cannot be ruled out at this resolution, but it is not part of the style.

**Figures**
- Small heads, big rounded torsos, tube arms. Figures are usually cropped at the waist and look positive and engaged.
- Glasses are common: 5 figures across 4 slides wear them.
- Viewpoints: eye level, from behind (cover, s8), or overhead (the s6 circle).
- One simple support line, such as a table edge (s1), is allowed.
- A scene can be reused across frames by swapping one element. The cover's whiteboard scene returns in s8 with the woman replaced by a mind map.

**Props and marks**
- Props: whiteboard, sticky notes (ink dashes, never words), laptop, book, cup, folder.
- Marks: burst ticks, a large `?`, mind-map ellipses and arrows.
- No speech bubbles.

**Garments that are white in real life** (shirts, thobes, kanduras, ghutras) are oat, as in the s7 white shirt and the collars in s1, s3 and s6. Paper shows through only in details cut out of ink.

**RULES**

1. **Palette.** Illustrations use only `paper`, `oat` and `ink`. No blue.
2. **Fills.** Every closed area is oat or ink. Paper appears only in details cut out of ink.
3. **Skin.** Skin is oat on every figure.
4. **Line weight.**
   - The outline is head height ÷ 22–25 at final size. Cover-scale figures measure 8–12 px on a 1920 slide.
   - Detail lines are 50–60% of the outline (5–7 px at cover scale).
   - A stroke's thinnest point is at least 60% of its thickest.
   - Caps are round.
5. **Contours.** Every figure has a closed silhouette and at least one inner line that stops short.
6. **Hair.**
   - Dark hair, beards and dark garments are solid ink.
   - Light hair is oat, with anything from no strands (short hair, back view) to many (curls, s2).
   - Strands are open strokes, not texture.
7. **Faces.**
   - Allowed: dot or dash eyes, a one-line nose, a one-curve mouth and C-shaped ears.
   - Brows and open mouths appear only for emotion.
   - No pupils, lashes or lips.
   - Glasses are open round or oval rims at detail-line weight. They may break the face outline, and eyes may be left out behind them (cover).
8. **Hands.** At least head-sized, with the correct number of separate fingers.
9. **Right hand.** The right hand gives, receives, eats, drinks, greets and points. Either hand may write: the reference's whiteboard writer (cover, s8) is left-handed.
10. **Flatness.** No shading, hatching, gradients, texture, cast shadows or scenery. A single support line such as a table edge is the only exception.
11. **Background blocks.** At most one oat background block, outlined on 2–3 partial edges.
12. **Fill spill.** Fill may spill up to about 2 line widths past hair, body and prop outlines. It never spills on faces or hands.
13. **Groups.**
    - A group reads as one oat mass. Ink goes on hair, ties, devices and at most one dark garment.
    - Alternating oat and ink garments (s4) is an option for rows seen from behind or in profile only.
14. **Nothing legible.** No text, numbers, logos, flags or religious symbols in the art.
15. **Placement.**
    - Art usually bleeds off the bottom or outer edge. A floating vignette (s1) is allowed.
    - Art never overlaps the callout or body text.
    - The top of the illustration group, counting any marks or board, sits about 26–28% down the slide. Heads usually sit 27–38% down.
    - At least 65% of the frame is bare paper.
16. **Direction.** Figures face into the frame, toward the headline or each other.
17. **No mirroring.** Figure art is never flipped. RTL versions are drawn separately (so the right hand stays the right hand).
18. **Restraint.** At most one illustration per frame, and not every page is illustrated.

## 4. Representation & culture

The reference shows only Western office dress. Masar's audience is 65.8% Gulf, so the figure set is extended using only the style's three moves: ink mass, oat mass and loose line. No patterns, gold or extra colours.

| Figure | How to draw it | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Shayla + abaya | Ink shayla around an oval oat face; ink abaya with an oat sliver at each wrist | Keep the face oval clean: no ear, no hair |
| Niqab | Ink shayla and niqab; an oat band at the eyes with dot eyes and brows | Mainly in Saudi and Qatari family scenes. Give her active roles, and never make her the only woman in a scene. |
| Egyptian hijab | Oat or ink wrap, higher and more structured, with 2–3 folds | An oat hijab can read as blonde hair. Close the outline under the chin; draw folds, not strands. |
| Thobe / kandura / dishdasha | An oat garment with 2 cuff lines. Collar: Saudi, Qatari. Tassel line: Emirati. Short tassel: Omani. | Keep firm outlines between face, ghutra and thobe so the oat areas stay separate |
| Ghutra + agal | Oat ghutra with a peaked fold; agal as 2 solid ink strokes; a red shemagh hinted with 3–4 ticks | Never a full check pattern. The ink agal gives the figure weight. |
| Omani headwear | Kumma: an oat pillbox with dots. Massar (turban): an oat wrap | The turban's Arabic name (مصر) is a different word from مسار |
| Egyptian student or parent | Oat polo or shirt, ink trousers, backpack. Some fathers have a moustache or stubble. | — |

**Modesty and contact**
- Every wrist gets a cuff line and every figure a neckline, because skin and cloth share one oat.
- Silhouettes stay chunky, hems reach the ankle, and seated feet stay flat.
- In Saudi-targeted pieces, adult women wear abaya and shayla (hair covered) by default. The niqab is one option among several.
- Unrelated men and women never touch. That rules out mixed hand-stacks (the s6 pose), handshakes and high-fives.
- Mixed groups form a spaced cluster around one object.
- Family contact is fine. The student leads; the parent supports.

**Group mix by market.** Build scenes from separate figure layers so each market's mix is a swap, not a redraw.

| Market | Student groups |
|---|---|
| Saudi Arabia, Kuwait, Qatar | Same gender. Mixed only in family, booth or crowd scenes. In Kuwait, women default to shayla or hijab. |
| UAE, Oman | Mixed, with spacing |
| Egypt | Mixed, with both veiled and unveiled girls |

**Never draw:**
- clichés: camels, dunes, lanterns, magic carpets, mashrabiya wallpaper, pyramids as shorthand for Egypt, gold, fake-Arabic fonts
- religious text or symbols: verses, "Allah", basmala, shahada, mosques, the Kaaba, pointed arches, prayer poses, crescent-and-star. Flyers get thrown away, and sacred text would go with them.
- national flags. Saudi law bans commercial use of the Saudi flag because it carries the shahada; treat all flags as off-limits.
- the left hand passing, receiving, eating or greeting; a finger pointed at a person; shoe soles or shoe-sole prints (use a dashed path or small walking figures instead)
- dogs in family scenes, wine glasses
- a veiled or bearded figure cast as the confused one by default
- real maps with borders or sea names (use an abstract metro-style route)
- real university logos or campus buildings

## 5. Typography

**Reference face: probably Garet** (Type Forward, free in Canva), Regular or Medium. This is **not confirmed**.
- Evidence: it was the best letter-width fit of the candidates tested, and its hooked y, flat-topped t, spurless two-storey a and single-storey g all match.
- Weight is uncertain. Garet's free Book weight renders lighter than the reference.
- To confirm, open the template in Canva and select the title; the font menu shows the face and weight.
- The callout and body text are too small to identify.

| Context | Latin | Arabic |
|---|---|---|
| Canva | Garet: letter spacing −90 (cover) or −100 (section titles), line spacing 1.0 | Alexandria |
| Web, code, PPTX | **Montserrat** variable at weight 450, the closest free match. Stem thickness and x-height to cap-height ratio match the reference. H, E and W are wider, and the y has a straight tail. | **Alexandria** 340 for display (a Google Fonts pairing for Montserrat). **Readex Pro** 325 for text, at 1.08–1.1× the Latin size. |

Check Garet's web-embedding licence before self-hosting it. *Proposed:* the condensed serif from the university proposal is retired in favour of this system.

| Role | Size on a 1920 slide | Weight | Line height | Latin letter spacing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cover title | 118–126 px | 450 | 1.0 | −0.07em (Garet −0.09) |
| Section title | 86–88 px | 450 | 1.0 | −0.065em (Garet −0.10) |
| Callout | 26 px (the reference is about 18 px, too small to project) | 400 | 1.5 | −0.01em |
| Body | at least 22 px | 400 | 1.55 | 0 |
| Pill | 18–20 px | 450 | 1 | −0.01em |

**Titles:** Title Case, 2–4 lines, start-aligned, ragged, never bold.

**Arabic**
- Letter spacing is always 0, because spacing breaks the joins between letters.
- Line height is 1.15–1.25 for display and 1.7–1.8 for body.
- Ragged, never justified. Emphasis comes from weight.
- Use Arabic punctuation (، ؛ ؟) and one digit system per piece.

## 6. Layout & components

**Slide grid (1920×1080, measured)**
- 104 px margins, two 805 px columns and a 102 px gutter.
- The top of the title's capitals sits at about 123 px.
- The callout and body text are pinned to the bottom, with the last line at about 968 px. The space between stays empty.
- The title's start edge lines up with the callout's outer stroke.

**Slide variants**

| Variant | Arrangement | Reference |
|---|---|---|
| Alternating (default) | Title and text in one column, art in the other. Swap sides on every slide. | s1–s3, s5, s6, s8 |
| Wide-title | A wide title across the top, art under it on the start side, callout at the bottom on the end side | s7 |
| Band | Title top-start, callout top-end, art in a band along the bottom, centred across both columns. Suits bilingual frames. | s4 |

**Cover**
- Title in column 2, with the top of its capitals about 20% of the way down. Art in column 1, bleeding off the bottom.
- Four pills: organisation top-start, date or city top-end, URL bottom-start (lined up with the title), email bottom-end.
- The cover's side margins measure about 70 px, possibly a mockup distortion. Use 104 px.

**Callout.** A rounded rectangle, one column wide.
- 1–2 px hairline stroke, 20 px corner radius, about 35 px side padding. No fill, no shadow.
- One or two real sentences, 3–4 lines, top-aligned in the box.
- Latin text is justified on slides and start-aligned on the web.
- Two optional sub-columns sit below it, flush with its outer edges, and hold the detail.

**Pills.** Capsules of a fixed width, about 337×41 px on a slide, with a hairline stroke, no fill and a centred label. Pills that are links or buttons on the web use `hairline-strong` or ink for the border.

**Logos (*Proposed*; the reference has none)**
- Cover: the Masar lockup replaces the top-start pill, and the GET ED × Eshra7ly lockup replaces the top-end pill. Both are single-colour ink, at least 40 px tall.
- Content slides: a small single-colour Masar lockup in the bottom-end margin, clear of the callout, 28–32 px tall.
- A partner university's logo goes top-end in its own colours. That frame has no blue element.
- Clear space around any logo is at least its own height. Logos never sit inside the art.

**Social formats.** For 4:5 and 9:16, put the text on top and let the art bleed off the bottom. Keep faces out of the bottom 25%.

**RTL**
- Mirror the layout, not the drawings. Text goes right, art goes left, and the pills swap sides.
- Use the `rtl` version of the art so figures still face the headline and act with the right hand.
- Routes, progress bars and arrows run right to left.
- Never flip logos, URLs, phone numbers or numerals. Wrap them in `<bdi>` or `dir="ltr"`.
- The lockup is مسار | Masar, with the Arabic on the right.

## 7. Production

**7.1 Workflow**
1. **Draw.**
   - Procreate: Studio Pen, pressure minimum 55–65%, StreamLine 40–60%. Set the brush size so a test stroke at final size measures head height ÷ 22–25. Procreate's size slider is a percentage, so measure the stroke rather than trusting the slider.
   - Or use a real brush pen and scan at 600 dpi.
   - Keep `fill` and `ink` on separate layers.
2. **Vectorise.** Use Illustrator Image Trace in Black and White, once per layer. For batches, use `potrace` or `vtracer`. Snap every colour to the three tokens and remove grey halos.
3. **Library.** Keep one Figma component per scene, with `ltr`/`rtl` and `L`/`M`/`S` variants. Export SVG, plus PNG@2x for Google Slides.

**7.2 SVG conventions**
- **Groups, from bottom to top:**
  1. `<g class="illo-fill" fill="#E5DAD0">`
  2. optional `<g class="illo-paper" fill="#F0F0F0">`
  3. `<g class="illo-ink" fill="#111111" fill-rule="evenodd">`

  Write the hex values into the SVG itself (as `fill` attributes) so Canva, PowerPoint and Figma render them. Add the classes for CSS.
- **Accessibility:** include a `<title>`, or `aria-hidden="true"` if the art is decorative.
- **Strokes:** convert them to filled shapes. Only line-only art (the path line, diagrams, icons) keeps live, round-capped strokes.
- **Naming:** `masar-illo_{scene-slug}_{L|M|S}_{ltr|rtl}_v{nn}.svg`.
- **Sizes:** each scene has three versions.
  - `L` is the full drawing. Never scale it below 35%.
  - `M` has 20% heavier lines and drops hair strands.
  - `S` has 50% heavier lines and keeps only the silhouette.
- **Minimum line weight:** 1.5 px on screen, 0.5 pt in print.

**7.3 AI generation**

Master prompt:
> Minimal editorial illustration of {SCENE}. Cast: {CAST}. Loose, confident black brush-pen line, near-even weight with a gentle swell, rounded ends, some inner contours left open. Chunky simplified figures: oversized shoulders and arms, small heads, big simple hands with separate fingers; minimal faces (dot eyes, one-line nose, one curved mouth line); some figures wear simple round glasses; some figures seen from behind. Exactly three flat colours: paper background #F0F0F0; one warm oat fill #E5DAD0 for skin and all light clothing, including white garments; near-black #111111 for lines and solid shapes (dark hair, dark garments, devices). No shading, no gradients, no texture, no scenery, lots of empty paper. {FRAMING, e.g. "waist-up, cropped by the bottom edge, left half of a 16:9 frame, facing right"}.

Negative prompt:
> extra colours, pastel palette, skin-tone variation, gradient, shading, cast shadow, cross-hatching, halftone, paper texture, grain, watercolour, 3D render, realistic anatomy, detailed eyes, anime, chibi, thin uniform technical line, sketchy construction lines, multiple overlapping strokes, background room, clutter, text, letters, numbers, logos, flags, religious symbols, watermark

**Tools**
- Recraft: native SVG output, palette lock, and a custom style built from up to 5 reference images.
- Midjourney with `--sref` for raster images.
- Use only Masar-owned seed masters as style references.

**Cleanup.** Snap the colours and trace every output, then fix these by hand:
- hands and finger counts
- faces
- ghutra folds
- stray text
- the left hand giving or receiving

**Records and ownership.** Log the prompt, model, seed and references for every image. Purely AI-generated images are not copyrightable (US Copyright Office, January 2025), so the recurring cast is drawn or substantially reworked by hand.

**The reference template's illustrations**

- **What Canva's licence bars:**
  - extracting its elements as standalone items
  - using them in logos or trademarks
  - any machine-learning or AI use (so no tracing them into prompts or training)
- **What it allows:** Canva designs containing its content in ads, print, websites, social posts and printed merchandise.

## 9. Checklist

**Colour**
- [ ] Only paper, oat and ink, plus at most one blue path element (none next to a partner logo).
- [ ] At least 65% bare paper; no gradients, shadows or halos.

**Figures**
- [ ] Oat skin, cuff lines and necklines on every figure.
- [ ] One line-weight scale throughout.
- [ ] The right hand gives, receives and greets. Finger counts are correct.
- [ ] No contact between unrelated men and women; the group mix fits the market.
- [ ] No religious symbols, flags, soles or clichés.
- [ ] Nothing legible in the art.

**Layout and type**
- [ ] Art never overlaps the callout or body text. It bleeds off an edge unless the frame is a deliberate floating vignette. Figures face the headline.
- [ ] Titles are regular weight. Latin letter spacing is tight; Arabic letter spacing is 0.
- [ ] Body text is at least 22 px on slides and 16 px on the web.
- [ ] RTL layout is mirrored and uses the `rtl` art. URLs and numbers stay left-to-right.
- [ ] Logos are single-colour and sit in their zones with clear space.

**Files and licensing**
- [ ] No Canva template elements.
- [ ] Licences logged; AI outputs logged and cleaned up by hand.
- [ ] Oat proofed in print against the paper you chose.
